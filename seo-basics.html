SEO Basics: How Search Engines Actually Decide Rankings

2026-02-107 min readBranket Solutions

SEO gets talked about like it's a trick — some secret setting you flip to appear on page one. In reality, it's closer to a checklist of things that make a page easier for search engines (and people) to understand, trust, and want to click on.

What search engines are actually trying to do

A search engine's job is to match a person's question with the page most likely to answer it well. That means ranking isn't just about keywords — it's about relevance, clarity, trust, and whether people who land on the page actually get what they came for.

The three things that matter most

1. Technical health

If a page loads slowly, breaks on mobile, or is hard for search engines to read, none of the rest matters much. This is the unglamorous plumbing — page speed, mobile-friendliness, clean URLs, no broken links.

2. On-page relevance

This is about whether the content on a page actually matches what someone typed. Clear titles, headings that reflect real questions people ask, and content that goes deep enough to be useful rather than padded out.

3. Trust signals

Other sites linking to yours, consistent business information across the web, and a track record of the site existing and being maintained all quietly build trust in a search engine's eyes.

Where to actually start

  • Make sure your site loads quickly and works properly on a phone
  • Write page titles and headings the way a customer would actually phrase a search
  • Claim and complete your Google Business Profile if you serve a local area
  • Publish content that answers real questions your customers ask you directly

What SEO isn't

It isn't a switch you flip once. It isn't stuffing a page with keywords. And it isn't something that shows results in a week — most meaningful movement takes a few months of consistent, honest effort. Anyone promising overnight rankings is usually setting you up for a penalty later, not a win now.

Done properly, SEO is closer to maintenance than magic — the reward is a website that keeps earning attention long after a single ad campaign would've stopped.
